# Divergence Moves

Loaded only from Phase 2, when the way is fogbound. One move per pass. Every move ends with its kill rule applied and feeds the meta-pattern scan. The moves' base mechanics are assumed known; what follows is the discipline that keeps them honest.

## Session rules

- Announce the move and the symptom that selected it before starting.
- Quantity before quality inside a move; judgment only at the kill step. Evaluating while generating kills the non-obvious candidates first.
- The kill step is public: every candidate is either developed or killed with a one-line reason, on the map. Nothing is quietly dropped.
- **Abandonment rule:** a move may produce nothing worth developing. Say so plainly — "this pass was barren" — record it, and either stop or switch moves with the user's consent. Forcing a survivor out of a barren pass is the one failure this file exists to prevent.
- Divergence output is candidate directions, never commitments. The user converges; the skill does not.

## Forced stimulus

*Symptom — no ideas, or every idea is a minor variation of the last one.*

Anchor on something genuinely unrelated to the domain — a "relevant" anchor reproduces the rut. List the anchor's own properties before touching the problem, then force a bridge from every property, including the awkward ones; the awkward bridges are where the non-obvious lives. Develop the 2–3 bridges with life in them; kill the rest, named.

Kill rule: a bridge that needed the anchor's property redefined into vagueness ("a beehive is... collaborative") was never a bridge — kill it and say why. Expect roughly 60–70% of bridges to die; a batch where nearly all survive was evaluated too gently.

## Purpose climb

*Symptom — a nagging sense the stated problem is not the real problem.*

Climb "what is this in service of?" two or three levels, until the purpose is one the user would defend without qualification. At each level, descend into 2–3 *other* children — different problems that would also serve that purpose. Then compare: is the original still the strongest child? Sometimes yes — say so and descend with confidence. Sometimes a sibling is stronger — present the reframe, but the user chooses the level to plan at.

Kill rule: siblings generated only to make the original look good are strawmen — each must be something a reasonable team might actually pursue. Name and kill any that aren't. Expect at least 1 of the 2–3 siblings per level to die on inspection; a level where every sibling survives was probably strawmen dressed up as options.

## Provocation

*Symptom — a constraint everyone treats as unbreakable, so every idea deforms around it.*

Confirm the wall is real, then state the impossible version flatly, as fact — never softened into something feasible; a feasible provocation is just an idea. Live inside it a few steps, then extract movement: from each consequence, the real mechanism that gets partway there. The provocation is scaffolding; the mechanism is the product. Discard the scaffolding.

Kill rule: a consequence yielding no extractable mechanism is recorded and dropped. Expect roughly half of extracted consequences to yield no mechanism. If the whole provocation yields none, the constraint is honestly load-bearing — write that in Bearings; it is a finding, not a failure.

## Flip hunt

*Symptom — the current approach feels inevitable; its assumptions have stopped being visible.*

Extract only load-bearing assumptions — the approach collapses if one is false; skip decorative ones. Flip each into its strongest *plausible* opposite, one a serious competitor could defend. Then the step that separates this from rhetoric — the hunt: name a real market, segment, product, or era where the flipped belief already holds. Real names only. No honest answer means the flip is dead — mark it dead; the original assumption approximates law here, which is itself worth knowing. Develop the flips with live answers, borrowing mechanisms from wherever the flip already works.

Kill rule: if every flip "turns out true somewhere", the flips were strawmen or the wheres were manufactured — redo the flips harder. Expect both halves as fixed expectations, not just possibilities: at least one flip dies for lack of a real "where", and at least one original assumption survives untouched.

## Verb hunt

*Symptom — the work is named as an artifact (a deck, a page, a report) and every idea is a variant of that artifact.*

Strip the noun. Write what the artifact must get whom to do, in what time — "get busy people to believe a cause-and-effect story and decide under uncertainty in 30 minutes". From the verbs, list the fields whose practitioners do that work, including one that would attack it. From each field take one method, tool, or raw material and bridge it to the problem. Develop the bridges that change what gets built; kill the rest, named.

Kill rule: a field whose contribution is a voice or a vocabulary and no method was a costume — kill it and say why. Expect about half the fields to die; a pass where every field survives took the title for the method.

## Meta-pattern scan

Mandatory close of every pass, even a barren one. Look across everything the pass produced — survivors and dead alike — and state one structural observation that no single candidate contains: a belief every candidate secretly shared, an axis all the dead ones died on, a constraint that shaped everything. One or two sentences, written into Bearings. The list is the output; the scan is the insight.
